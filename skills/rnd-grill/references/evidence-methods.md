# EVIDENCE METHODS

Read only the methods needed for the requested uncertainty. None requires a saved artifact or another method first. Use the shared discussion rules for material choices and [saving](saving.md) if a durable result is requested.

## DEPENDENT QUESTIONS

For a broad goal, identify the destination, exclusions, precise questions and real prerequisites. Discuss the next answerable independent questions together; dependent choices wait for evidence or answers. Keep in-scope questions not yet precise distinct from excluded work. A short list in dialogue is enough; do not create a map, numbered decisions or claim/status machinery merely to investigate.

When resuming existing maps or typed decision records, retain their paths, identities, questions, answers, dependencies and history. Do not claim another worker's item, reset its state, renumber records or silently convert decision records into implementation tickets. Reconcile unclear ownership or prerequisites before dependent work. Saving an approved conclusion does not authorize rewriting its source map or ticket.

## RESEARCH

Inspect relevant code, callers, tests and recorded decisions. For external facts, prefer primary sources that own the claim; cite the supporting path and location or stable direct link. Record versions or dates when currency matters. Distinguish source contracts from executed observations, user statements and inference. Missing access or a failed fetch is unknown, not proof of absence. Attribute secondary sources and their limits when the primary source is unavailable.

Preserve conflicting evidence and qualifications. State the question, scope, attributable findings, assumptions, unknowns, limits and next discriminating source or check. User priorities remain choices, not research findings. Do not expose private input to an external source without authorization.

## OPPORTUNITY SURVEY

Survey only when that is the request; do not audit every discussion. Establish the scope and trace representative behavior across meaningful modules, responsibilities, callers and tests. State sampled or inaccessible areas. Look for knowledge spread across callers, fragile responsibilities, repeated change pressure or difficult verification at interfaces; size and dependency graphs alone do not establish a problem.

For supported opportunities, name the code/callers, observed pressure, possible direction, consequences, relative effort, risk and uncertainty. Rank observed impact or frequency against cost and risk, explaining the order without forcing a count. A reasoned no-change result is valid only within the inspected coverage. Ranking candidates does not select one or approve its design.

## SELECTED-BOUNDARY DESIGN

Establish the selected module, responsibility or seam; ask if materially ambiguous rather than choosing an architecture-wide target. Trace actual caller needs, ownership, invariants, ordering, errors and relevant performance. An interface includes all caller obligations, not only signatures; a useful seam changes behavior without changing callers.

Compare real alternatives, including keeping the current design when viable. Assess responsibilities, locality, caller burden, migration impact, failure paths and verification at the public interface. Depth means useful behavior behind a small interface, not fewer lines. Use design principles to ask evidence-backed questions; do not introduce layers, dependency injection or speculative abstractions merely to satisfy them.

Pressure-test choices with concrete use, failure and change scenarios. Distinguish agreed responsibilities and interface from proposals, rejected alternatives, assumptions and unresolved ownership. An open design is not ready implementation work. Design approval grants no product edits or test deletion; later test replacement/deletion needs applicable explicit confirmation and passing checks through the new interface.
