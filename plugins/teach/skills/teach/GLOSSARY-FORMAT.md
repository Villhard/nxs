# GLOSSARY.md

The settled vocabulary of the topic. Lessons, exercises, and learning records all use these words and no synonyms. Writing an entry is itself a test: compressing a concept into two sentences is evidence the user holds it.

## Template

```md
# {Topic} glossary

{A sentence on what this topic covers.}

## Terms

**Hypertrophy**:
Muscle growth driven by mechanical tension and metabolic stress across repeated sessions.
_Avoid_: bulking, getting big

**Progressive overload**:
Raising the demand on a muscle over time through load, volume, or intensity.
_Avoid_: pushing harder, levelling up

**RPE**:
A 1 to 10 self-rating of how hard a set felt, where 10 is failure and 8 leaves two reps
in the tank.
_Avoid_: effort score, intensity rating
```

## Rules

- **Promote a term only once the user understands it.** This is a record of compressed knowledge, not a dictionary to read for learning. A concept met ten minutes ago waits until they can use it correctly.
- **Pick one word and say so.** Where the field uses several names, choose the best and list the rest under `_Avoid_`. That choice is what makes the language compress.
- **Two sentences at most.** Define what the term is, not what it does or how to perform it.
- **Use glossary terms inside definitions.** Terms defined in terms of each other are what make the hard ones land later.
- **Group when clusters appear** under subheadings such as `## Anatomy`. A flat list is fine while the terms cohere.
- **Resolve loose usage explicitly.** If the wider field uses a word two ways, state which one holds here: "a set always means a working set; warm-ups are counted separately".
- **Update in place.** A week-one definition can be wrong by week six. Rewrite it rather than leaving both.
