# PRACTICE FORMATS

The catalogue practice is chosen from. Without one, every practice turns into the same
blank-sheet task, because that is the only format that invents itself.

Each entry names what the learner produces, where the feedback comes from, and the signal
that calls for it. The sources are here so the catalogue can be argued with rather than
believed.

## HOW TO CHOOSE ONE

- **Tie it to something open.** Practice is issued only against a line in `OBSERVATIONS.md`
  or against theory just taught. The tie is written into the task itself.
- **Teach first.** Never issue practice whose theory has not been given or confirmed as
  already held. A link to a neighbouring lesson is not theory.
- **Use an allowed source.** Theory may come from the linked entry in `RESOURCES.md` or the
  exact supplied file in `materials/`. Missing sources follow the search, defer, or agreed
  unsourced choice in `SKILL.md`.
- **Size the current step for about 85 per cent success**, so roughly one attempt in six fails.
  This is a practice-sizing heuristic from `SKILL.md`, not a criterion for closing an
  observation or plan node.
- **Novice in unfamiliar ground:** worked example, then completion, then whole task with
  support fading out. Handing a novice the full task is where copying starts.
- **Fading support beats standing support** once the learner succeeds. Support goes back up
  after an observed failure and comes down after an observed success, never on a schedule.
- **Expertise reversal.** Detailed guidance helps a novice and gets in the way of someone who
  already performs. Once a skill is there, cut the support and reach for transfer.
  (Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler, Sweller 2003, Educational Psychologist 38(1).)
- **Practice before theory only in one case:** the base is already there and the goal is to
  grasp a principle. Then format 9, and the explanation lands on top of the learner's own
  attempts.

## UNDERSTANDING AGAINST FLUENCY

Two different jobs, and they need different formats.

Understanding is built by a new task the learner has not met. Fluency is built by repeating a
primitive until it comes out without being reconstructed each time. The signal that separates
them is one sentence from the learner: they follow the explanation and still cannot produce
the thing without looking it up. That signal is the same in code, on a barbell, at an
instrument, and in a language.

Fluency work is an addition to whole tasks and never a replacement for them.

## THE FORMATS

**1. RECALL PROBE.** Produces an answer from memory with the material closed. Feedback is the
comparison against the source, immediately after the attempt. Reach for it when the unit is due
under the date-based spacing rule in `SKILL.md` and has not been retested. Retrieval beats
rereading by a wide margin.
([Adesope, Trevisan, Sundararajan 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654316689306))

**2. WORKED EXAMPLE.** Produces a marked-up solution: each step, and the reason it is there.
Feedback is the comparison against the principle behind the solution. Reach for it when the
topic is new and the learner has nothing to build the first attempt from. The effect size
behind this is a mathematics-only meta-analysis; treat it as weaker evidence outside that
domain until a broader source turns up.
([Barbieri et al. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1))

**3. COMPLETION.** Produces the missing steps of a solution that is otherwise finished.
Feedback is local: the gap either closes or it does not. Reach for it as the step between a
worked example and a whole task, and whenever the whole task would collapse into copying.
([van Merrienboer, 4C/ID](https://www.4cid.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/vanmerrienboer-4cid-overview-of-main-design-principles-2021.pdf))

**4. ERROR HUNT.** Produces a located defect, an explanation of it, and a fix. Feedback is the
failing check turning green, plus the name of the misconception behind the error. Reach for it
to train reading someone else's work, and to attack a misconception the learner has shown.
The gain shows up on a delayed test and is invisible on an immediate one, so do not judge the
format by how the session felt.
([McLaren et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-015-0064-x))

**5. PREDICT, RUN, EXPLAIN.** Produces a written prediction with its reasoning, then the
observed result, then an account of the difference. Feedback comes from reality: a program
run, a demonstration, a lift on video. Reach for it when the learner holds a rule that is real
but attached to the wrong thing. The prediction has to be recorded before the run, or the
format collapses into hindsight.
([Predict-observe-explain, NZCER](https://arbs.nzcer.org.nz/predict-observe-explain-poe))

**6. TRANSFER.** Produces the same move applied to a surface the learner has not seen: another
library, another domain, another instrument. Feedback is whether the transfer holds, plus the
question of which feature they leaned on. Reach for it once a skill is green in its original
setting, to find out whether it was the skill or the setting.
([Barnett and Ceci 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12081085/))

**7. WHOLE TASK WITH FADING SUPPORT.** Produces a complete piece of work of a simplified
difficulty class, with support that is removed from one task to the next. Feedback is the
acceptance criteria of the whole task. Reach for it as the default once the ladder above has
been climbed. Support means a skeleton, a checklist, a partial solution, a coach standing
there; it comes off in that order.
([van de Pol, Volman, Beishuizen 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-010-9127-6))

**8. PART-TASK DRILL.** Produces the same primitive over and over, against a threshold of
speed and accuracy. Feedback is the threshold. Reach for it on the fluency signal above, and
never as a substitute for whole tasks. Scales, a lift's setup, conjugations, the shape of a
declaration.
([van Merrienboer, 4C/ID](https://www.4cid.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/vanmerrienboer-4cid-overview-of-main-design-principles-2021.pdf))

**9. INVENTION BEFORE INSTRUCTION.** Produces as many candidate solutions as the learner can
generate for a problem whose method they have not been taught, failed attempts included.
Feedback is the canonical explanation afterwards, laid against what they tried. Reach for it
when the goal is a principle rather than a procedure, and only when the learner already has a
base to invent from. The effect reverses for younger learners and for domain-general skills -
do not reach for this format there even with a base in place.
([Sinha and Kapur 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105))

**10. MASTERY CHECK.** Produces 5 to 8 new tasks in chat, with the material and lookup closed,
and allows at most one error. Feedback is the threshold, not a score. Reach for it before
closing a plan node. This is the check `SKILL.md`'s THE NEXT STEP points to; its dates, failure
handling, and chat-only state are defined there.
([Kulik, Kulik, Bangert-Drowns 1990](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543060002265))

## TWO ADD-ONS

These attach to any format above rather than standing alone.

- **Explain your own solution.** Ask why each step is correct, before the next one. It is what
  keeps fading from producing solutions the learner cannot account for. Not a default on top of
  a worked example: paired that way it has been shown to get in the way instead of helping, so
  reach for it once the learner is producing solutions of their own, not while they are still
  reading someone else's.
  ([Bisra et al. 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x);
  worked-example caveat from [Barbieri et al. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1))
- **Come back to it later.** The same task returned to after a gap, rather than repeated the
  same day. The gap grows with how long the material has to survive.
  ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CPVWR2006.html))

Mixing several skills inside one practice session helps in some places and hurts in others:
strong for telling categories apart, weaker in mathematics, and worse than blocking for
vocabulary pairs. Treat it as a setting to choose per topic.
([Brunmair and Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf))

## EXTENDING THE CATALOGUE

A format invented for one course lives in that course's `NOTES.md`. It moves here after it has
worked twice, and it arrives in the same shape as the entries above: what the learner
produces, where feedback comes from, when to reach for it.
